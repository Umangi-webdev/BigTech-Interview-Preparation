## 🔄 Understanding the Interview Process

Many software engineering hiring processes include some combination of the following stages.

### Round 1: Application and Resume Screening

**Purpose:** Evaluate whether your qualifications match the position.

What to prepare:

- A clear, ATS-friendly resume.
- Relevant projects and internship experience.
- Programming languages and technical skills.
- Academic background and achievements.
- A portfolio or GitHub profile, where relevant.

**Preparation tip:** Be ready to explain every project, skill, and achievement listed on your resume.

### Round 2: Online Assessment

**Purpose:** Evaluate coding ability, problem-solving, and sometimes aptitude or other role-specific skills.

Common topics:

- Arrays and strings.
- Hash maps and sets.
- Sorting and searching.
- Binary search.
- Recursion and backtracking.
- Trees and graphs.
- Dynamic programming.
- Time and space complexity.

Some assessments may also include aptitude, SQL, debugging, or multiple-choice questions.

### Round 3: Technical Coding Interview

**Purpose:** Understand how you approach programming problems and communicate your reasoning.

Interviewers may evaluate:

- Understanding of the problem.
- Choice of algorithm and data structure.
- Correctness of the solution.
- Time and space complexity.
- Edge cases.
- Code readability.
- Ability to improve an initial solution.

**Best practice:** Explain your approach before coding, test your solution with examples, and discuss its complexity.

### Round 4: Computer Science Fundamentals

Depending on the position, interviewers may ask about:

- Object-Oriented Programming.
- Operating Systems.
- Database Management Systems.
- SQL and database design.
- Computer Networks.
- Programming language fundamentals.
- Concurrency and multithreading.

### Round 5: System Design or Low-Level Design

This stage is more common for experienced roles, although some entry-level and internship interviews may include basic design questions.

Possible topics:

- URL shortener.
- Chat application.
- Video conferencing application.
- Notification service.
- Rate limiter.
- Library management system.
- Parking lot system.
- Object-oriented design principles.

### Round 6: Behavioral Interview

**Purpose:** Understand how you work with people, solve problems, handle challenges, and take responsibility.

Common questions:

- Tell me about yourself.
- Describe a challenging project.
- Tell me about a mistake you made.
- Describe a disagreement with a teammate.
- Tell me about a time you learned something quickly.
- Why do you want to join this company?
- What is your greatest strength?

Use the **STAR method**:

- **S — Situation:** Explain the context.
- **T — Task:** Describe your responsibility.
- **A — Action:** Explain what you did.
- **R — Result:** Describe the outcome.

### Round 7: Hiring Manager or Final Discussion

Depending on the company, this may cover:

- Project ownership.
- Technical decision-making.
- Teamwork.
- Role expectations.
- Motivation and career goals.
- Questions about the team or position.

Not every company uses all these stages, and their order can differ.

- Hash maps and sets.
- Linked lists.
- Stacks and queues.
- Binary search.
- Sorting algorithms.
- Recursion and backtracking.
- Trees and binary search trees.
- Heaps and priority queues.
- Graphs and graph traversal.
- Greedy algorithms.
- Dynamic programming.
- Time and space complexity.

# 🗣️ Behavioral Interview Preparation

Technical knowledge alone may not be sufficient. Interviewers can also evaluate communication, teamwork, ownership, decision-making, and learning ability.

## Frequently Asked Behavioral Questions

1. Tell me about yourself.
2. Why do you want to join this company?
3. Describe your most challenging project.
4. Tell me about a technical problem you solved.
5. Describe a mistake and what you learned from it.
6. Tell me about a disagreement with a teammate.
7. How do you handle deadlines?
8. Describe a time you learned a new technology.
9. What are your strengths and weaknesses?
10. Why should we hire you?

## Use the STAR Method

| Component | Meaning | What to Explain |
|---|---|---|
| Situation | Context | What was happening? |
| Task | Responsibility | What did you need to accomplish? |
| Action | Your contribution | What steps did you take? |
| Result | Outcome | What happened, and what did you learn? |

### Behavioral Preparation Tips

- Use real experiences instead of memorized stories.
- Explain your personal contribution to team projects.
- Be honest about mistakes and limitations.
- Include measurable results only when they are accurate.
- Connect your experiences to the position you are targeting.

# 📅 30-Day Preparation Roadmap

This roadmap is a starting point for candidates who already have basic programming knowledge.

| Days | Focus Area | Suggested Activities |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Arrays and Strings | Solve basic and intermediate problems |
| 4–5 | Hash Maps and Sets | Practice frequency counting and lookup problems |
| 6–7 | Linked Lists | Reverse, merge, and detect cycles |
| 8–9 | Stacks and Queues | Solve common stack and queue problems |
| 10–11 | Binary Search and Sorting | Practice searching and ordering problems |
| 12–14 | Trees and Binary Search Trees | Traversals, depth, and tree properties |
| 15–16 | Heaps and Priority Queues | Top-K and scheduling problems |
| 17–19 | Graphs | BFS, DFS, shortest-path fundamentals |
| 20–22 | Recursion, Backtracking, and DP | Solve representative problems |
| 23–24 | OOP and Core CS | Revise OOP, OS, DBMS, and CN |
| 25–26 | SQL and Databases | Joins, subqueries, indexes, and transactions |
| 27 | System Design Fundamentals | APIs, caching, databases, and scaling |
| 28 | Behavioral Preparation | Prepare STAR stories and project explanations |
| 29 | Mock Interview | Complete a timed coding and technical session |
| 30 | Company-Specific Revision | Review the target job description and weak areas |

### Daily Routine

A possible daily schedule:

- 60 minutes: DSA practice.
- 30 minutes: Review previous problems.
- 30 minutes: CS fundamentals.
- 20 minutes: Project or behavioral preparation.
- 10 minutes: Record mistakes and revision notes.

Adjust this schedule to your college workload, placement timeline, and current skill level.

# 📖 How to Use This Repository

Follow this process for an organized preparation experience:

1. **Choose a target company.** Read its official job description and eligibility criteria.
2. **Understand the role.** Determine whether you are applying for an internship, graduate role, or experienced position.

5. **Use trusted resources.** Prioritize official company guidance and reputable learning platforms.
6. **Practice under interview conditions.** Explain solutions, test edge cases, and analyze complexity.

8. **Practice behavioral questions.** Use genuine experiences and the STAR framework.
9. **Review mistakes.** Maintain notes on incorrect solutions and concepts that need revision.
